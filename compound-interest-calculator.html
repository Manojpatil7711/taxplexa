Compound Interest Calculator

See how savings or investments grow with monthly deposits.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest means you earn interest on your earlier interest. This calculator adds interest every month and then adds your monthly deposit, so you can see how steady saving builds over time.

The result is an estimate. Real returns vary, and investment returns are not guaranteed.

How this compound interest calculator works

Each month, the calculator applies one-twelfth of the annual interest rate to the current balance, then adds your monthly deposit at the end of that month. It assumes the rate stays constant for the full period.

Monthly calculation: New balance = Previous balance × (1 + annual rate ÷ 12) + monthly deposit.

Worked example

Starting amount: 10,000; monthly deposit: 0; annual nominal rate: 10%; period: 3 years; monthly compounding. Estimated future value: 13,481.82; estimated interest: 3,481.82 (in the same currency units as the starting amount).

This is a mathematical illustration, not a promised return. The calculator does not include tax, fees, inflation or changes in the interest rate. It does not model a bank account’s specific terms or investment risk.

Frequently asked questions

How is monthly interest calculated?

The annual nominal rate is divided by 12. Interest is added to the balance each month, then the monthly deposit is added at the end of the month.

Does this estimate include tax, fees or inflation?

No. The result excludes tax, account or investment fees, inflation and any change in the interest rate. Actual returns may be lower or higher.

Are investment returns guaranteed?

No. This is a mathematical estimate only, not a promise or recommendation. Check the terms and risks of the product you use.

Compound interest calculator: understand long-term growth

What compounding means

Compound interest is interest calculated on the current balance, including interest credited earlier. When returns remain invested, each period can earn returns on a larger balance. Regular deposits can add to this effect, although actual savings and investment products may use different crediting rules.

How to use the calculator

  1. Starting amount: enter the money already saved or invested.
  2. Monthly deposit: enter the additional amount you plan to add each month. Enter zero if you will make no regular contributions.
  3. Annual interest rate: use the annual nominal rate as a percentage. Do not enter a monthly rate here.
  4. Years: enter the period you want to model, then calculate.

Review the estimated future value and estimated interest separately. The future value includes your deposits; the interest figure represents the modelled growth above the money contributed.

Formula and example

With monthly compounding and deposits made at month-end, the monthly rate is the annual nominal rate divided by 12. Each month: new balance = previous balance × (1 + monthly rate) + monthly deposit. For example, 10,000 invested for 3 years at a constant 10% nominal annual rate, with no further deposits, grows to approximately 13,481.82 before tax, fees and inflation. This is an illustration, not a promised return.

What changes the result?

A higher rate, longer period or larger regular deposit generally increases the projected balance. Try changing one input at a time to see its effect. Starting early can matter because growth has more time to compound. However, a smooth fixed rate is only a modelling assumption; market investments can rise or fall and bank rates can change.

Important limitations

The estimate does not account for tax, platform or fund fees, inflation, withdrawals, changing rates, market volatility or product-specific deposit timing. It is not investment advice and does not compare risk. Check the provider's terms, deposit protection rules and investment risks before making a decision.
